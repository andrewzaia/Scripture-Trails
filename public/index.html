          Rooted in fifteen years of Scripture study.

          Scripture Trails is the culmination of about fifteen years of comprehensive personal study of Scripture across the Old and New Testaments.

          The Bible is the authoritative foundation of the project, and its studies are anchored in biblical passages. Manuscript research, archaeological findings and historical evidence from around the world provide supporting evidence and context for understanding the biblical text.

          This study continues. The project grows through continued reading and research, adapting to new learning as archaeological discoveries, newly discovered or re-examined manuscripts and further scholarship deepen understanding. Findings are assessed in context, and interpretations are revisited where the evidence warrants.

          A study tool, not a replacement for Scripture.

          Scripture Trails lets you read an ESV, NASB20, NIV or NKJV passage, follow verse references and explore editorially selected connections. The atlas now uses a substantially expanded OpenBible.info cross-reference network, alongside separately labelled editorial links. It explores up to 10 hops using bounded samples; not every possible path appears at once.

          How connections are labelled

          Quotation: a New Testament author quotes or cites another passage. Parallel: similar accounts or teaching. Echo: shared imagery or wording. Theme: a human-curated thematic association. Additional imported links are labelled as dataset references, not automatically classified as quotation or prophecy. Context links only move to neighbouring verses when no cross-reference is known.

          History and interpretation

          The Bible is the primary text for biblical claims. Historical background, authorship, dating and interpretative explanations should separately identify their evidence and uncertainty. Connected verses do not automatically establish a particular theological reading.

          Gospel journey map

          The separately accessible Journeys of Jesus map draws its episodes from the Gospels and Acts, with a Bible citation for each event. It displays repeated episodes in the same town and has a draggable event timeline. Locations are modern approximate coordinates or clearly indicated regional placeholders, and drawn connectors do not assert a historical walking route. Order across different Gospel accounts is an editorial study reconstruction.

          Data and privacy

          Saved passages and notes are kept in your browser's local storage; there is no account or cloud sync. ESV text is requested from Crossway; NASB20, NIV and NKJV text is requested from API.Bible through server-side proxies. Bible text and API keys are never included in the downloadable source. API.Bible passages use its Fair Use Management System to report displayed Scripture, including anonymous device and session identifiers. This reporting does not include your study notes.

          Optional website analytics

          If you choose to allow analytics, Scripture Trails uses Google Analytics 4 to count visits to broad site areas, such as the Bible reader, Atlas and journey maps. We do not send Bible reference searches, reading passages, personal notes, highlights or notebook contents as event parameters. Google Analytics may use cookies and collect technical information about your visit. Tracking is off until you choose to enable it, and it is restricted to the official www.scripturetrails.com domain. You can change your choice at any time.
