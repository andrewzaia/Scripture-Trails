# Person-centred Scripture trail editorial record

Updated: 28 October 2026. Release: 0.18.0.

The library expands from eight to twenty trails: the original forty verse anchors remain, and twelve additional figures contribute forty-eight contextual passage ranges. These are selective editorial reading aids, not full biographies, harmonised chronologies, externally peer-reviewed histories or archaeological verification. Each stage records its primary Scripture reference and editorial review status. No publisher Bible text is bundled in the dataset.

## Method and limitations

New summaries are short, original paraphrases of what the selected narrative presents. AI-assisted textual checks consulted selected primary chapter views and passage results from Bible Gateway and USCCB. This is not an independent human review or a verse-by-verse source-critical audit. USCCB's displayed translation and verse divisions can differ from the reader editions; application references are checked against the site's canonical navigation metadata. No USCCB translation is added to the app. Live reading continues through the existing authorised publisher APIs.

The original eight trails retain their verse references and notes. All twenty now expose the same explicit source and review labels. Automated checks validate profile identity, canonical reference boundaries, unique stages, request-size limits and metadata; they cannot establish historical accuracy or theological interpretation.

## Specific context cautions

- Hannah's story is not a universal promise concerning childbirth.
- Samuel's episodes do not flatten the book's differing presentations of kingship.
- Elijah and Elisha remain distinct people; miracle reports are described as narrative claims.
- Jeremiah 29 addresses an exiled community, and the trail is not a chronological diary.
- Daniel's three companions in chapter 3 are not misidentified as Daniel; disputed royal identities remain unresolved.
- Nehemiah 8 includes Ezra and the community and is not converted into a modern territorial claim.
- Mary's profile is specifically Jesus' mother. Peter's Matthew 16 reading does not settle denominational questions.
- John the Baptist is not John the apostle. Mark 6 is explicitly described as a retrospective account.
- Acts 15's dispute does not supply a complete later biography or a definitive moral verdict on either leader.
