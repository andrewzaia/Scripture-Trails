# Third Pauline journey and Rome voyage - Scripture study editorial pass

Reviewed on: 2026-10-31
Scope: 22 third-journey stages (Acts 18:23-21:17) and 22 prisoner-voyage stages (Acts 27:1-28:31), completing original reading-context material for all 83 Pauline stages.

## Source method

Canonical Scripture is the primary narrative source. The study notes explain episodes in Acts in their immediate narrative sequence and add clickable canonical cross-readings, not text extracted from proprietary translations. All original stage identifiers, map pins and source licences are retained. No new absolute dates, fabricated intermediate ports, exact boat routes or unverified archaeological markers are introduced.

The route between Assos and Troas is treated carefully: Acts 20:13 reports that Paul travelled to Assos on foot while companions sailed, and Acts 20:15 distinguishes sailing opposite Chios from arriving at Samos. Acts 20:17 locates Paul's farewell speech at Miletus, where Ephesian elders join him, rather than at Ephesus. Acts 20:2-3 names Greece, not Corinth, in this visit.

Acts 20:22-24, 21:4 and 21:10-14 record Paul's resolve and the disciples' concern about Jerusalem. The published discussion by Alan J. Thompson in [Acts commentary](https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/commentary/acts/) identifies the difficulty of reconciling different descriptions of the Spirit's involvement. That commentary is interpretive and should not be represented as external evidence that the event happened.

Acts 27 distinguishes arrival at Fair Havens from the proposed Phoenix winter harbour, a sheltered passage past Cauda from an island landing, and the feared Syrtis from a destination. Soundings, offshore drift and the unnamed wreck bay remain unpinned; Malta is identifiable as an island, not a verified exact beach or wreck site. A separate [Warren Carter, 'Aquatic Display: Navigating the Roman Imperial World in Acts 27', New Testament Studies 62 (2016), 79-96](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688515000284) offers peer-reviewed literary interpretation of the storm and Roman power. Only the published abstract was assessed, not the complete article. This academic treatment is not a verified maritime reconstruction.

## Limits and acceptance

The output is AI-assisted original narrative commentary and canonical cross-reading, not independent archaeological, textual or historical specialist signoff. The total Pauline content-depth coverage is 83/83. The 79 Gospel events still require further evidence work. The wider v1.0 launch has 12 open criteria, including publisher rights, external translation-provider behaviour and independent critical-claims assessment.

Verification required before publication: normal npm semantic-version release generator, full build and data tests, browser suite, PR-wide release validation and commit-matched Cloudflare deployment with a live footer and study-content smoke check.
