# Gospel itinerary narrative context and targeted source review - 31 October 2026

## Scope and method

All 79 Gospel itinerary entries now contain individual original editorial reading-context paragraphs and 156 navigable canonical comparison passages. The canon is the primary source for the Gospel narratives themselves. Commentary compares what the biblical texts say, considers the surrounding literary contexts and explicitly avoids converting ambiguous place and chronology descriptions into independently verified maps.

**This is a full coverage pass of editorial reading depth, not a full 79-event historical verification.** The existing evidence register remains a first-pass and selected high-risk topics remain open. There has been no independent specialist review, original manuscript collation or archaeological site certification.

## Targeted research and evidence boundaries

- Birth and census: Luke 2:1-2 invokes a census connected with Quirinius, while Matthew 2:1 places Jesus' birth in Herod's reign. Conventional historical dates create a chronology tension. Leroy A. Huizenga, ["Bethlehem and the Census", *The Oxford Handbook of Christmas* (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780198831464.013.17), publisher abstract accessed. This abstract identifies competing historical and grammatical interpretations, not a settled year or proof of a lost census. Full chapter not accessed.
- The end of Mark: The publisher's [ESV manuscript footnote on Mark 16:9-20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark.16.9-Mark.16.20&version=ESV) confirms a documented longer-ending textual variation. The Gospel itinerary cites Mark 16:1-8 at the empty tomb; it does not state that the longer ending lacks a history in other witnesses.
- Ascension: Luke 24:50-53 and Acts 1:6-12 share a broad Bethany/Mount of Olives setting but do not identify a surveyed site. A. W. Zwiep, ["The Text of the Ascension Narratives (Luke 24.50-3; Acts 1.1-2, 9-11)", *New Testament Studies* 42 (1996), 219-244](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688500020713), publisher extract accessed. The extract describes critical-edition support for longer readings in Luke 24:51-52. This does not substitute for a fresh primary manuscript comparison, and the full article was not accessed.

The previously recorded Nazareth encounter, Bethany anointing, temple action, parallel fisherman calls, Galilee crossings, Jericho healing details and other uncertainties remain visible. They have not been collapsed into invented historically dated individual visits.
