# Twenty prayer, faith, hope and Hezekiah assessments - 29 October 2026

## Scope and method

Batch `connections-20-05` adds twenty previously unassessed pairs: the seventeen remaining New Testament comparisons in the existing register and three Hezekiah parallels. Coverage becomes 108 of the unchanged 115 explained pairs. Seven remain outside the dated register: ce086 and ce090-ce095. Seventy-six assessed pairs include named commentary or limited publisher-summary support; this is a coverage measure, not independent scholarly certification.

Each record has two checked publisher passage citations and one scoped named commentary citation. Seventeen commentary works by sixteen authors support selected claims. Seven narrative parallels and thirteen thematic links retain their classifications; no quotation, echo or typology classification is added. The 125 earlier assessments and 233 existing source entries are preserved.

The Bible is the authoritative foundation of Scripture Trails. These are original editorial comparisons, with a commentator credited only for the claim stated in the citation. Christian lectionary and evangelical perspectives are identified. No full literature review, independent specialist signoff, manuscript collation or translation-equivalence audit is claimed.

## Consequential distinctions retained

- Matthew and Luke have different prayer forms and settings. Mark retains an appreciative scribe and the Jewish setting of the commandment discussion. The review does not construct a single verbatim account.
- Acts 9 and 22 retain different companion-hearing language, narrative voices and uses of Ananias. Both belong to Acts. The selected commentary addresses Acts 9 without being represented as a complete textual harmonisation. Stephen retells Exodus within a particular rhetorical defence.
- Grace and good works remain connected in their actual contexts. John, Romans, Ephesians and Galatians are not interchangeable formulations; the faith-in/faithfulness-of-Christ discussion, authorship and fuller theological debates remain open.
- Resurrection hope is not a claim that bodily life is evil, that grief should end immediately or that a complete eschatological timetable has been established. The chosen John 11 range precedes the emergence of Lazarus. Hebrews and Paul do not become instructions to reject evidence.
- The providence comparison retains harmful human intent and Joseph's reassurance; it does not excuse abuse or require unsafe trust. The love comparison likewise retains truth and active care.
- Hezekiah's reform summaries, the reported Assyrian defeat and the illness narratives retain differing scope and ordering. Biblical overlap does not independently verify casualty totals, a plague diagnosis, an instrument or astronomical mechanism. Isaiah's poem and later placement of the remedy/sign question remain visible.
