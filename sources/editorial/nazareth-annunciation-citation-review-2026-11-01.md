# Nazareth Annunciation citation review - 1 November 2026

Scope: City dossier `nazareth` in `public/data/city-studies.json` at v0.44.13. This is an editorial source-provenance correction, not an independent archaeological review or verification of a biblical miracle.

## Issue
The Annunciation traditions finding cited only the general landing page of Ken Dark's `Archaeology of Jesus' Nazareth`, whose book abstract primarily describes the separate Sisters of Nazareth site. That general book synopsis is an incomplete match for a specific Annunciation compound assertion.

## Sources checked and their limits
1. Ken Dark, "Pilgrims, monks, and digs", `Archaeology of Jesus' Nazareth`, Oxford University Press, 2023, chapter 2, pp. 23-51, DOI 10.1093/oso/9780192865397.003.0002, https://academic.oup.com/book/45361/chapter-abstract/389285524 . The accessible publisher abstract directly names archaeological research at the Church of the Annunciation. **Full chapter not accessed**.
2. Franciscan Custody of the Holy Land, "Nazareth - Basilica of the Annunciation", https://www.custodia.org/en/sanctuaries/nazareth-basilica-of-the-annunciation/ . This site's own custodial history reports the sanctuary's commemoration, archaeology and later building phases. It is a primary account of **devotional tradition and custodianship**, not independent proof of the location of Luke 1's reported meeting.

## Correction
Use both direct citations in the city research bibliography, the Annunciation finding, related pilgrimage timeline entry, and the detailed interpretive study. The finding now explicitly distinguishes religious commemoration and excavated building phases from historical identification of Luke 1's precise event setting. Retain the prior broader Nazareth studies where appropriate to early Roman village settlement.
