# Twenty Old Testament narrative assessments - 29 October 2026

## Scope and method

Batch `connections-20-04` adds twenty previously unassessed narrative parallels. Coverage becomes 88 of the unchanged 115 explained pairs; 27 remain outside the dated register. Fifty-six assessed pairs include named commentary or limited publisher-summary support. These figures measure editorial coverage. They do not measure independent scholarly agreement or historical certification.

Every new assessment has two checked publisher passage contexts and one named commentary citation with a claim locator. Six commentary works by four authors support the batch. Seven assessments use one of the two commentaries available only through indexed excerpts; this limitation is recorded on those source entries. All twenty pairs remain narrative parallels. The other 105 assessments and 190 existing source entries are preserved without alteration.

The Bible is the authoritative foundation of Scripture Trails. Narrative overlap and differences are described in original editorial prose. A commentator is credited only for the stated claim, not automatically for the entire comparison or caution. The selected commentaries are Christian lectionary or conservative evangelical works. Their confessional interpretations, authorship proposals, external historical claims, cited print works and chronological charts do not become independently established findings here.

ESV chapter views also contain neighbouring chapters; the checked 1 Kings 9 view includes the selected Kings 8 ranges and is labelled accordingly. USCCB retrieval was unavailable or inconsistent for several of these chapters. ESV numbering is explicit for 2 Kings 12, where edition chapter boundaries differ.

Only the declared ranges support each assessment. Publisher notes or commentary were not substituted for missing passage text. No manuscript collation, translation-equivalence audit or complete account harmonisation is claimed. No publisher passage or commentary text is stored in the repository: only source links, bibliographic metadata, original summaries, locators and limitations. Public access was not treated as redistribution permission.

## Retained differences and next work

The reviews retain the tribute report in Kings, priest/Levite and musical differences, Saul's added evaluation, different narrative placements, the discipline clause in Samuel, the distinct prayer introductions, qualified royal evaluations and names, and the additional priestly confrontation in Uzziah's story. Biblical source notices do not establish that their named works survive. Shared narrative wording does not establish independent witnesses, a settled date or archaeological verification.

The queen remains unnamed; modern romances or identities are not inferred. The land-healing promise is kept in its Solomon/Israel/temple setting. Skin-disease terminology does not establish a modern diagnosis, and Azariah the king is distinguished from Azariah the priest. These are selected textual comparisons, with fuller literature, textual questions and independent specialist review still open.
