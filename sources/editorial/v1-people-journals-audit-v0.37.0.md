# v1.0 flagship audit - person trails and guided study journals

Audit date: 2026-10-31
Prepared website release: v0.37.0
Scope: 20 Scripture person trails and six guided journals. AI-assisted biblical-text editorial assessment, not independent specialist signoff.

## Inventory reviewed

The twenty existing person trails contain 88 passages, each with a selected canonical reference. Their original step IDs, order, original notes and source status remain unchanged. Eight trails initially contained five selected stages and twelve contained four. Previously the earlier figures were mostly represented by one-sentence notes that were insufficient as standalone study material.

All twenty now include a unique contextual study, a comparison of specific scriptural arguments, a reflection question, a scope limitation and three additional clickable canonical passage references. This adds sixty supplementary references, without expanding the original stage timelines or changing the person's identity used by the index. The supplementary readings open in the licensed Scripture reader and starting verse in the Atlas.

The six existing journals already had six stages each (36 in total), with structured context, questions and reading order, more than 200 stage readings, and approximate study times. They now have distinct, extended essays explaining what the journal examines, which interpretations need special care, and how to compare its sources. The creation, Messiah, shepherd, covenant, grace and comfort paths retain their canonical anchors, exact progress IDs, completed-reference membership, stage reference order and approximate durations.

## Evidence and interpretation issues addressed

- Abraham: distinguish the original Genesis family narrative from later Pauline and Jacobean uses of Genesis 15:6.
- Moses: avoid treating liberation, covenant and later leadership scenes as a single undifferentiated speech or a dated route map.
- David and Solomon: retain prophetic judgement and moral failure alongside dynastic and Temple traditions.
- Hezekiah: distinguish the wording and aims of the biblical siege narrative from Assyrian royal inscription claims. Their overlap does not independently establish a miracle.
- Esther: retain the Persian court setting as a feature of the biblical story, not independent proof of each character. The composition and historical status of the narrative are debated.
- Daniel: distinguish Daniel's absence from chapter 3's three-companion episode from the Daniel 6 lions' den narrative and the later visionary chapters; royal identifications and composition dates remain debated.
- Ruth, Hannah, Samuel, Elijah and Elisha: preserve the voices of vulnerable people, literary setting and social obligations; narrative miracles are not medical guarantees.
- Jeremiah: retain the communal and exilic setting of Jeremiah 29:11 and the new-covenant audience of Israel and Judah.
- Nehemiah: include ordinary labourers and social reforms, not solely governor-centred rebuilding.
- Mary, Peter, John the Baptist and Barnabas: separate individual identities, sources and literary sequences instead of assuming complete reconstructed biographies.
- Paul and Jesus: distinguish a sequence selected from Acts or the Gospels from independently dated trips or a harmonised modern chronology.
- Guided journals: clarify original prophetic audiences, typological and literal fulfilment, poetic and apocalyptic genres, and the different faith-and-works arguments of Paul and James.

## Scoped external research consulted

The Bible's canonical texts provide the primary narrative claims. The following named research is linked as additional context where public claims of independently settled history would be particularly misleading:

- Tawny Holm, **Book of Daniel**, Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Religion, 2019, https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62249/chapter-abstract/551358644 . Accessible abstract distinguishes Daniel's court tales and later apocalyptic visions, and discusses a likely later final composition. Full text is access-limited.
- **Daniel 3 and 6**, Yale Bible Study, https://yalebiblestudy.org/courses/daniel/lessons/daniel-3-and-6-study-guide/ . Public guide notes that Daniel does not appear in chapter 3, which concerns his three companions.
- Shaul Shaked, **Esther, Book of**, Encyclopaedia Iranica, https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/esther-book-of/ . Discusses Persian setting, literary form and dating of the text without verifying individual incidents.

These sources are contextual and non-exclusive. Claims about events involving other people remain framed as biblical accounts and are not newly certified by these three scholarly references. A complete cross-tradition textual and historical review, and independent specialist scrutiny, remain open.

## Validation and compatibility

- Require all twenty individual studies and sixty supplementary references to resolve against canonical verse counts.
- Check unique refs, HTTPS research citations, explicit limits, HTML links and no change to original progress anchors.
- Require every journal guide to include a substantive literary overview, textual distinction and reading practice.
- Run GitHub source and browser suites, version/changelog guards and production deployment verification for the exact merge commit.
- Keep the v1.0 flagship content audit **open**: Gospel and Pauline journeys, broader people and place source audits and high-impact historical claims still require review.

No manual QA work is assigned to the project owner. Inaccessible device-specific or specialist validation is documented as a limitation, not described as completed.
