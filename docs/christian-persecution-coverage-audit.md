# Christian persecution geographic coverage audit

Checked: 2026-11-11
Latest candidate release: v1.9.0

The archive now includes 392 source-attributed records across 73 countries and territories. This release adds 34 reports, introduces 15 previously unrepresented countries, and adds further distinct reports for three countries already represented. The number is a count of curated published entries, not a measure of prevalence, risk or severity.

## Coverage and attribution method

- The reports capture both dated incidents and clearly labelled continuing government restrictions. Do not label a policy as a newly occurring physical attack.
- Record actual event dates at source-supported precision; do not invent exact days. Attribute reported responsibility, procedural outcomes and unresolved motives.
- Explicitly qualify criminal damage to church property where investigations found no religious motive.
- Do not duplicate a single attack under multiple publishers or create multiple event totals for a single series without an incident-group identifier.
- World heat intensity counts published records. Unshaded countries are not evidence of an absence of persecution or violence.
- Each country needs a supported geometry code; do not infer incident coordinates from a country name or a broader region.

## New geographical coverage in this release

Bhutan, Brazil, Brunei, Chile, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Lebanon, Morocco, Saudi Arabia, Tajikistan, Tanzania, Tunisia, Uzbekistan and Venezuela.

Further new cases are recorded in Israel, Mozambique and the Philippines. Existing Ukraine and prior international catalogue entries are retained.

## Still not represented: research queue

Examples requiring specific source research: Oman, Qatar, Bahrain, United Arab Emirates, Maldives, Kuwait, Argentina, Peru, Bolivia, Guatemala, Honduras, El Salvador, Uruguay, Paraguay, Costa Rica, Panama, Chad, Ghana, Malawi, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Botswana, Angola, Burundi, Rwanda, Madagascar, Eswatini, Armenia, Georgia, Serbia, Kosovo, Croatia, Romania, Greece, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Netherlands and Ireland. Also consider states with only one or two published entries, which are highly underrepresented.

Some small island states and disputed territories are absent as geometries from the locally hosted Natural Earth 110m map. For instance, Maldives is missing: do not silently add a new country to the report dataset without addressing geometry validation and country-level map display. Lack of a country record does not mean that persecution never occurred.

## Future sourcing priority

Prefer concrete episodes, dated official findings, independently documented witness accounts or publisher-attributed claims. If the evidence concerns rights restrictions rather than violence, state exactly that, and retain any documented countervailing official practice (for example, private worship being permitted). Expand one-country catalogues before claiming broad country coverage.
