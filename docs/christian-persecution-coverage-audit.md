## Inclusion and counting

Published reports, including single-source accounts and disputed allegations, remain eligible for inclusion without independently establishing the motive or the perpetrator's identity.

Every record retains its publisher and source URL. An OSCE annual reporting summary is one catalogue report, not automatically each of the incidents mentioned inside that summary. Where separately documented sites or dates identify distinct events, those events may be separately catalogued with a shared incident group. Multiple victims in one event are not counted as separate reports.

Report counts and map shading reflect the number of catalogue entries, not independently verified persecutions, casualty totals or persecution rates. Unmapped countries are not necessarily incident-free. Locality pins are created only when approximate place data can be grounded; nationwide or unspecified-location reports contribute to their country shading.

## daesh-linked Christian persecution: scope and caveats

The previous 45 reports include the 2014 Mosul ultimatum, the occupation of Christian Nineveh Plains towns, forced conversion, captivity, sexual violence and the repurposing of distinct churches as prisons; the Khabur Assyrian hostage crisis, Christian killings and al-Qaryatayn monastery destruction in Syria; two further executions of Christians reported in Libya; church and pilgrim attacks in Egypt; the Dapchi abduction and later West Africa affiliate attacks in Nigeria; claimed attacks in North Kivu; the Mozambique insurgency; and the Marawi cathedral and Catholic Mass attacks.

The archive records published events and publisher-attributed claims, not a complete register of all atrocities. The existing 2014-era and more recent reports remain intact. Related entries share incident_group where they derive from the same campaign. Some geographically broad descriptions cannot be placed at street or village scale. Country shading counts report records and is not a measure of unique attacks, victims or risk per capita.

The coverage includes a limited number of events from groups linked or alleged to be linked to daesh. An affiliate's claim is not treated as proof of an independently established chain of command. Reporting about boko haram predating its 2015 affiliation, al-shabaab, or armed groups without a grounded link to daesh is not automatically attributed to daesh. A retrospective source discussing insurgency operations before affiliation is identified accordingly.

Editorial copy uses lowercase names for terrorist organisations. Formal publisher names, links, quotations and identifiers may retain their source spelling where fidelity requires it. Locally stored photographs are added only with established reproduction rights; citations and external source pages are not a blanket licence.

### Further v1.14.0 additions and attribution cautions

The latest batch distinguishes the 24 January 2016 Qamishli restaurant bombing, 19 June 2016 Assyrian Sayfo commemoration suicide attack and 11 July 2019 church car bombing. The January allegation of daesh responsibility was disputed, the June attack was unclaimed and the 2019 claim was unconfirmed. These cases belong in an inclusive Christian persecution reporting archive but must not be described as proven daesh-directed operations.

The new eastern Congo records document separately dated raids from 2020 to July 2026, including Christian churches, aid facilities, worship leaders, conversions alleged in perpetrators' messaging and villages. Nigeria additions include the 2020 aid worker killings with an anti-Christian-conversion message, and an October 2020 allegation of abduction after religious screening. Source qualifications preserve unknown victims' religious identities and distinguish claimed responsibility from established acts.

Published report totals do not equal unique attacks, victims, casualty figures or prevalence. Localities lacking grounded coordinates remain included in country-level map shading without fabricated village pins. Prior multi-incident summaries can overlap narrower dated entries and must not be double-counted as distinct deaths.

### v1.15.0 Mozambique and Khabur source details

Open Doors Australia described Christian homes, churches, a pastor's family and religious books damaged or displaced at Muanquina on 30 July 2025, followed by reports of fifteen Christians beheaded in Marera on 1 August and nine relatives of a Christian youth worker abducted in Naphela on 3 August. The same publication includes a late-July anti-Christian conversion threat and an execution claim in Intutupue; Open Doors cautioned that those victims' Christian religious identities were not independently established. July-only dating is retained for an unspecified pastor abduction.

The separate November 2025 Memba campaign in Chipene and Lurio is not merged with the existing September-October Memba attacks. Agenzia Fides reports concerning Tel Shamiran hostage-taking and damaged Christian symbols in the Khabur valley remain grouped with the existing 2015 Khabur campaign, not treated as new victim counts. These source records improve granularity, not an independently verified total of unique attacks or people affected.

### v1.16.0 wider geographic and category coverage

This batch adds independently identifiable publications on public worship restrictions, detention of congregations and clergy, legal punishment for religious practice, church damage, mob attacks and violence affecting Christian communities. Reports are attributed to Forum 18, Human Rights Watch, USCIRF, the UK Home Office, Amnesty International, local religious-freedom monitors, news services and Nigerian police.

Records from Zikke and Yelewata involve predominantly Christian farming communities, but sources do not establish that every victim was Christian or that religion was the only motive. The 2026 China Jiaquan investigation, 2024-2026 Shreider prosecution and January 2025 Seethwaka intimidation and stone damage share incident groups where the same underlying case has multiple reported stages. An Iranian convert's 2025 sentencing cross-references her 2024 arrest. Russian prosecutions and Christmas arrests in Iran are reported aggregates, not invented distinct victim counts.

Dates are year/month/day precise only when the source gives them. An October 2026 publication about Cuba describes a detention on 26 September. Neither an event's later publication nor an official allegation is treated as independent evidence of its occurrence or the identity of a perpetrator.

Map shading reflects the count of report records, not a measure of unique attacks, victims, prevalence or independently verified religious motivation. Sites without grounded locations contribute to country totals without fabricated pins. This release does not republish unlicensed media.
