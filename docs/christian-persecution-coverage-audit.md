# Christian persecution geographic coverage audit

Checked: 2026-11-11
Candidate release: v1.12.0

The catalogue now contains 505 published source-attributed report records across 91 countries and territories. This release adds 45 daesh-related records across Iraq (16), Syria (8), Libya (2), Egypt (3), Nigeria (5), the Democratic Republic of the Congo (3), Mozambique (6) and the Philippines (2). The previous release added 25 records and seven countries: Sweden, Netherlands, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Argentina, Georgia and Serbia.

## Inclusion and counting

Published reports, including single-source accounts and disputed allegations, remain eligible for inclusion without independently establishing the motive or the perpetrator's identity.

Every record retains its publisher and source URL. An OSCE annual reporting summary is one catalogue report, not automatically each of the incidents mentioned inside that summary. Where separately documented sites or dates identify distinct events, those events may be separately catalogued with a shared incident group. Multiple victims in one event are not counted as separate reports.

Report counts and map shading reflect the number of catalogue entries, not independently verified persecutions, casualty totals or persecution rates. Unmapped countries are not necessarily incident-free. Locality pins are created only when approximate place data can be grounded; nationwide or unspecified-location reports contribute to their country shading.

## daesh-linked Christian persecution: scope and caveats

The 45 new reports include the 2014 Mosul ultimatum, the occupation of Christian Nineveh Plains towns, forced conversion, captivity, sexual violence and the repurposing of distinct churches as prisons; the Khabur Assyrian hostage crisis, Christian killings and al-Qaryatayn monastery destruction in Syria; two further executions of Christians reported in Libya; church and pilgrim attacks in Egypt; the Dapchi abduction and later West Africa affiliate attacks in Nigeria; claimed attacks in North Kivu; the Mozambique insurgency; and the Marawi cathedral and Catholic Mass attacks.

The archive records published events and publisher-attributed claims, not a complete register of all atrocities. The existing 2014-era and more recent reports remain intact. Related entries share incident_group where they derive from the same campaign. Some geographically broad descriptions cannot be placed at street or village scale. Country shading counts report records and is not a measure of unique attacks, victims or risk per capita.

The coverage includes a limited number of events from groups linked or alleged to be linked to daesh. An affiliate's claim is not treated as proof of an independently established chain of command. Reporting about boko haram predating its 2015 affiliation, al-shabaab, or armed groups without a grounded link to daesh is not automatically attributed to daesh. A retrospective source discussing insurgency operations before affiliation is identified accordingly.

Editorial copy uses lowercase names for terrorist organisations. Formal publisher names, links, quotations and identifiers may retain their source spelling where fidelity requires it. Locally stored photographs are added only with established reproduction rights; citations and external source pages are not a blanket licence.

### Remaining daesh research priorities

Review the full UN investigative dossiers and other primary reporting for additional distinct sites in Mosul, Qaraqosh, Bartella and Karamlesh; the Armenian church and other appropriated churches; the Assyrian Khabur villages and Raqqa; more local Egyptian and Nigerian cases; and specific Mozambique and eastern Congo attacks with clear dated reports. Investigate other attributed affiliate attacks without merging them with unrelated militant groups or assuming every victim was Christian.

## Remaining zero-report research queue

Investigate Romania, Zimbabwe, Honduras, Iceland, Norway, Ireland, Denmark, Austria, Switzerland, Belgium, Bulgaria, North Macedonia, Montenegro, Slovenia, Czechia, Finland, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Moldova, Angola, Zambia, Rwanda, Burundi, Namibia, Papua New Guinea and other unrepresented territories.

Small countries including Bahrain and Maldives are missing from the current 110m map geometry. Do not create country shading for them by reusing a neighbouring country polygon. Incorporating correct geometry is a separate mapping task.

## Further depth

Prioritise low-coverage countries including Haiti, Afghanistan, South Sudan, the Philippines, Yemen, Libya and Morocco. Continue consulting church statements, local broadcasters, OSCE/ODIHR, human rights monitors, historical reporting and published social-media accounts, identifying each claim as reported.

Do not download or publish third-party photographs without reproduction rights.
